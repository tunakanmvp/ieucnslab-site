**Stanford EE261 The Fourier Transform and its Applications** is an undergraduate course taught at Stanford by Prof. Brad Osgood. It is published for free on [Stanford Engineering Everywhere](https://see.stanford.edu/Course/EE261) with videos, course notes, assignments and exams.

**What's in it?** Fourier series, the Fourier transform and its properties, convolution, sampling, the discrete Fourier transform and FFT, linear systems and the higher-dimensional Fourier transform. Thirty lectures in all.

**Why we recommend it:** Where Oppenheim's course builds the general framework of signal processing, Osgood's devotes a whole term to a single tool, the Fourier transform. With his witty delivery and physical examples, it's ideal for anyone who wants to anchor the ideas in our [Fourier post](/en/blog/fourier-transform/) in solid maths. The course notes were later published as a book.

**Who is it for?** Anyone with calculus and basic linear algebra who wants to learn Fourier analysis in depth.
