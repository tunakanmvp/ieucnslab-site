**Stanford EE263 Introduction to Linear Dynamical Systems** is a course taught at Stanford by Prof. Stephen Boyd. The lecture videos, notes, assignments and exams are free on [Stanford Engineering Everywhere](https://see.stanford.edu/Course/EE263).

**What's in it?** Applied linear algebra, least-squares and least-norm solutions, state-space models, the matrix exponential, stability analysis through eigenvalues, the singular value decomposition, controllability and observability.

**Why we recommend it:** Where Oppenheim's course describes systems through their input and output signals, Boyd's looks "inside" the same systems: state variables and matrices. With examples from circuits, signal processing, communications and control, it shows the full engineering power of the idea in our [linearity post](/en/blog/linearity/). Boyd's energetic, witty delivery is a bonus.

**Who is it for?** Engineering students with basic linear algebra. A background in control or signals isn't required, but makes the course more enjoyable.
