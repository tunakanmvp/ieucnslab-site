**Stanford CS229 Machine Learning**, taught at Stanford by Andrew Ng, is one of the best-known courses in the field. The 2018 lecture videos are on Stanford Online's YouTube channel, and the lecture notes and problem sets are free at [cs229.stanford.edu](https://cs229.stanford.edu/).

**What's in it?** Linear and logistic regression, support vector machines, decision trees and ensemble methods, neural networks, model selection and cross-validation, independent component analysis (ICA) and reinforcement learning.

**Why we recommend it:** The maths behind the methods whose intuition we covered in our [SVM](/en/blog/what-is-svm/), [Random Forest](/en/blog/what-is-random-forest/) and [XGBoost](/en/blog/what-is-xgboost/) posts is derived here step by step. There's also a lecture on ICA, which we use to clean artefacts from EEG.

**Who is it for?** Anyone with probability, linear algebra and calculus who wants machine learning to stop being a black box. It's a maths-heavy course.
