**MIT 18.06 Linear Algebra**, taught at MIT by Prof. Gilbert Strang, is one of the most watched maths courses in the world. Videos, problem sets and exams with solutions are free on [MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06-linear-algebra-spring-2010/).

**What's in it?** Systems of linear equations, matrices, vector spaces, orthogonality and least squares, determinants, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and the singular value decomposition (SVD).

**Why we recommend it:** Linear algebra is the language of biosignal processing and machine learning. A multichannel EEG recording is a matrix; cleaning artefacts with ICA, reducing dimensions with PCA or training a neural network are all matrix operations. Strang builds these ideas through their meaning before their formulas. The "Fourier of a graph" in our [graph theory post](/en/blog/graph-theory/) is really an eigenvector story too.

**Who is it for?** Anyone with high-school maths. The videos were recorded in 1999, but the teaching hasn't aged a day.
